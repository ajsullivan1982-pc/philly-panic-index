The Sixers are 2–0. The vibes are at a record high. The Risk Desk Has Doubts.

PPI Risk Desk Oct 23, 2026 2 min read

Two games into the 2026–2027 season, the Philadelphia 76ers have proven two things thus far: they are capable of beating good teams, and they can do it in the most panic-inducing way possible.

We have watched the LeBron-era team knock out the Knicks and the Cavs already. Thursday night's victory was an emotional roller coaster, with the team erasing a 29-point deficit to win 114–106, marking the largest comeback in franchise history.

While our colleagues in the Department of Irrational Confidence have already started requesting PTO for the inevitable parade down Broad Street, the Risk Desk has requested further evidence before reserving its spot on the parade route.

1. The Defense

Opening night was encouraging. The Sixers held the Knicks to just 100 points, created difficult possessions, and showed evidence that this roster is built to compete defensively.

Against Cleveland, however, Philadelphia gave up 69 points in the first half before limiting them to 37 in the second.

It must have been quite the rousing halftime speech by Nick Nurse. Whatever adjustments were made, the first-half defense had our investors ready to throw in the towel.

The ability to make such adjustments helped quell some in-game anxiety and quiet doubts before they could become season-long predictions. The necessity of making those adjustments, however, remains a concern.

2. Ball Handling

Turnovers were another tale of two games.

Game 1: 10 total.
Game 2: 11 in the first 13 minutes.

Poor ball handling and offensive miscues helped Cleveland build a massive first-half advantage. While the offense eventually got its act together, we still consider this a metric worth tracking and a Key Panic Indicator going forward.

We do not believe that an elite offense can routinely donate possessions to the opposition and expect the defense to pick up the tab.

3. Star Power

Thursday night, Maxey scored 32, Embiid 24, Brown 21, and LeBron had 17 points and 10 rebounds.

The roster is stacked enough this season to overcome mistakes that would leave most teams waiting for the final buzzer.

The Sixers will need to rely heavily on good health and continued execution at an elite level. While we love a good offensive performance at PPI, the Desk will be closely monitoring to make sure that the star power of this Sixers squad isn't concealing some underlying structural problems.

Final Assessment

Positives: Defensive adjustments, second-half execution, offensive firepower.

Risks worth watching: Early-game turnovers, consistency, and dependence on dramatic comebacks.

We give credit where credit is due. But the Risk Desk would prefer to congratulate the Sixers for avoiding a 29-point deficit rather than overcoming one.
