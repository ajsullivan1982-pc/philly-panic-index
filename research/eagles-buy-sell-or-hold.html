Eagles: Buy, Sell or Hold?

PPI Risk Desk Oct 28, 2026 4 min read 
Asset Status: DISTRESSED
Record: 2–5 | Five consecutive losses
Outlook: NEGATIVE

Five straight losses, the latest a 38–21 beating from Dallas on Monday night. The franchise quarterback is expected to miss the rest of the season, and the injury report has gotten long enough that the Desk has started tracking the healthy players instead.

With the November 10 trade deadline approaching, nobody needs convincing that the Eagles are in trouble. That matter has been settled.

What to do about it is another story.

The Risk Desk has been asked to weigh three options: buy, sell or hold. The Futures Desk was also consulted, although its recent forecasts have not inspired much confidence.

BUY: One last run at the division

There’s an argument for buying. The NFC East is a mess, and despite everything, the Eagles are not mathematically out of it.

But the shopping list keeps getting longer. The offensive line has been gutted by departures and injuries. The top two receivers sat out Monday. The defense just let Dallas put up 38. And Tanner McKee, whatever his eventual ceiling, is running an offense that wasn’t convincing even before half the roster disappeared.

One good offensive lineman doesn’t fix that. Another receiver doesn’t either.

The Desk has made preliminary inquiries into the availability of an entire healthy roster. No offers have been received.

Spending future picks to rescue a 2–5 team without its starting quarterback is a hard sell to the board.

Risk Desk assessment: Too much money chasing too little certainty.

SELL: Accept the losses and prepare for 2027

This is probably the most emotionally satisfying option, and five straight losses will do that to a person. Trade the veterans, collect the picks, move up the draft board and forget this season ever happened.

The catch is that selling only works if somebody is willing to pay.

Several of the Eagles’ most valuable players are hurt or recovering. Saquon came back Monday from a hamstring and left with a bad back. Opposing front offices can read an injury report too, and they know exactly how desperate this looks.

There is also a difference between moving a player who doesn’t fit the future and moving one because sports radio has reached its daily operating temperature.

The front office already banked a future second-rounder in the Jurgens deal. If someone wants to overpay for a veteran, the Desk will gladly take the meeting. Turning useful players into late-round picks to prove management is awake is something else entirely.

Risk Desk assessment: Take calls. Don’t hold a clearance sale.

HOLD: The least satisfying option

The Desk recognizes that recommending patience to Eagles fans after five straight losses will do nothing for our approval ratings.

Nevertheless.

Jalen Hurts is expected to make a full recovery. The roster still has real talent under contract. Whenever he comes back, he’ll need a functioning football team waiting for him, and tearing it down now just hands 2027 a new set of problems.

Then there’s the NFC East, which keeps doing its part to leave the door open. The division is available. The Eagles have shown remarkably little interest in claiming it.

Holding doesn’t mean standing still forever. The coaching staff needs to be evaluated, and so does McKee. So does a defense that made a two-win Dallas team look like contenders. If the losing keeps up, the front office will eventually have to admit the current approach isn’t working.

For now, the Desk would settle for better decisions over more transactions.

Risk Desk assessment: Hold the core. Evaluate everything else.

Final Assessment

BUY: We don’t see a realistic acquisition that fixes enough of what’s wrong.

SELL: Fine in theory. Less attractive when half your trade chips are injured and everybody knows you’re desperate.

HOLD: Not exciting, but probably the correct choice.

RISK DESK VERDICT: HOLD - NEGATIVE OUTLOOK

The Desk understands the frustration. Five consecutive losses are not a temporary market fluctuation, and there are fair questions about where this organization is headed.

But the worst move a panicking team can make is a panic move.

The Risk Desk will revisit its recommendation as conditions deteriorate, or improve. We are advised improvement remains technically possible. The Futures Desk declined to comment.
